Key Cross-Economy Insight

Credit risk models behave fundamentally differently across credit infrastructures. In traditional bureau systems (CIBIL), trade-line depth and historical repayment trajectories yield high linear separability (ROC-AUC 0.836, KS 0.54). In unsecured peer-to-peer lending (LendingClub), higher borrower variance requires dual-model voting and calibrated decision thresholds.

Economic & Empirical Findings

1. Linear Separability in Indian Bureau Data

CIBIL data features extensive trade-line history (time_since_recent_payment, num_times_delinquent, etc.) which demonstrates strong linear separability. The linear hyperplane provides a high KS distance (0.5403) and robust precision (67.14%).

2. Policy-Level Thresholding in US P2P Data

In LendingClub, defaulting loans represent a minority class under high variance. Using standard 0.50 threshold yields low recall. Calibrating the threshold to 0.13 increases default recall to 66.96%, preventing costly uncollateralized defaults.

3. Scaling Asymmetry

The LendingClub Random Forest model was trained directly on unscaled continuous features with 40 dummy indicators, while Logistic Regression requires continuous standard scaling to converge properly without distorting binary dummy indicators.
